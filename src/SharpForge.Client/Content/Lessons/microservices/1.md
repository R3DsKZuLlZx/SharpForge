## Welcome to Microservices with .NET!

In this course you'll learn how to design, build, and operate a microservices system using .NET and the broader cloud-native ecosystem. By the end you'll have the skills to decompose a monolith, containerize services, communicate between them, and deploy to Kubernetes with observability built in.

This first lesson sets the stage: what microservices are, when they make sense, and the trade-offs you'll encounter.

## What Are Microservices?

Microservices are an architectural style where a system is composed of **small, autonomous services** that each:

- Focus on a **single business capability**
- Own their **own data store**
- Communicate over **lightweight protocols** (HTTP, gRPC, messaging)
- Are **independently deployable** and scalable

## Benefits of Microservices

- **Independent deployment** — Ship one service without redeploying the entire system.
- **Team autonomy** — Small teams own a service end-to-end.
- **Scalability** — Scale only the services that need it.
- **Resilience** — A failure in one service doesn't bring down the whole system (when designed well).
- **Technology flexibility** — Each service can choose its own stack.

## The Trade-Offs

Microservices are not free. They introduce significant operational complexity:

- **Distributed systems are hard** — Network failures, latency, and partial failures are the norm.
- **Data consistency** — No shared database means eventual consistency and patterns like Sagas.
- **Observability** — A single request may touch many services; you need distributed tracing.
- **Testing** — Integration and end-to-end testing across services is more difficult.
- **DevOps maturity required** — You need CI/CD, container orchestration, and monitoring in place.

<div class="warning-box">
    <div class="warning-box-header"><span>⚠️ Important</span></div>
    <p>If your team doesn't have automated deployments and monitoring today, start there before adopting microservices. The architecture amplifies both good and bad operational practices.</p>
</div>

## When to Use Microservices

Microservices are a good fit when:

- The system is **large and complex** with many independent features.
- Multiple **teams** need to work on the system simultaneously.
- Parts of the system have **different scaling requirements**.
- You need **independent deployment** for faster release cycles.
- Your organization has the **operational maturity** to manage distributed systems.

## When to Stay with a Monolith

- You're a **small team** (fewer than 5–8 developers).
- The domain is **not well understood** yet — boundaries will shift.
- You don't have **CI/CD** and **container infrastructure** in place.
- **Simplicity** is more valuable than flexibility at this stage.
